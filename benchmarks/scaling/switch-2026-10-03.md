# History-switch review, 0.4.0

**BLOCKED: do not merge or tag this head.** A full-history prefix followed by the
round-one bounded estimator is implemented, but no fitted legacy prefix meets the
selection budget. The provisional startup-only switch of 8 fails both required
long-budget quality gates. No reservoir, weighting, EI-count, or quality-threshold
tuning is included in this round.

All compilation, tests, measurements, and numerical aggregation run on an AWS
c6i.2xlarge Linux spot machine (8 vCPU, GCC 13.3). Timing probes share the machine;
these are contended measurements, not isolated peak-throughput claims. The branch
is rebased onto 0.3.1 (`13885b9`); engine and codegen gitlinks are unchanged.

## Switch selection

The applied linear budget is 375 microseconds at 64D, or 5.859375 microseconds
per dimension: **93.75 microseconds at 16D**. The budget is not increased.
With the legacy ten-observation random startup, the largest qualifying power of
two is **N_s = 8**, below 4,096. It is a startup-only value, not evidence that a
fitted full-history model is affordable. Even forcing fitting with one observation
costs 227.65 microseconds at 16D.

Below `history_switch`, estimation, EI draws, pending-liar handling, and proposal
RNG consumption stay on the exact 0.3.0 path. A successful finite `tell()` increments
the completed usable observation count, then activates bounded retention at N_s.
Issued, outstanding, failed/abandoned proposals, worker count, and wall time never
advance the switch. Reset restores the exact-prefix state.

After the switch, retain 25 elites, 64 recent non-elites, and 448 separately seeded
older non-elites. Keep elite-triggered good-model refresh, 32-completion bad-model
epochs, 513-point density tables, live pending overlays, and eight EI draws.
`--tpe-history-switch N` and StudySpec `sampler.config.history_switch` override the
positive uint64 threshold. Larger thresholds increase prefix memory and ask cost.

## Round-one fixes retained

Dense `OrdinalSet` membership applies through cardinality 100,000,000; larger
spaces retain the disk fallback. A rerun with one million unique pseudo-random
ordinals measures 0.0120772 us/insert and 15,464 KiB peak RSS for dense storage,
versus 3.07594 us/insert and 3,536 KiB for disk. This isolates insertion; it does
not claim a corresponding end-to-end trial speedup.

Archive insertion and writer enqueue remain atomic after the backpressure wait.
Trial-timeout archive/stream replay, bounded 50-ms polling, stalled-reader stop,
complete-line deadline/SIGTERM behavior, and cached immutable stream availability
remain covered. The quality ctest timeout stays 300 s. Changelog 0.3.0 is released,
and 0.4.0 remains above rebased 0.3.1.

## Evidence and reproduction

Compact public CSVs and verified adjacent metadata sidecars are under
`benchmarks/optuna/evidence/2026-10-03-switch-{quality,ask,native,ordinal,identity}.csv`.
They include per-seed regrets, trace/binary/source hashes, budget and configuration,
the benchmark-only full-history patch, platform/compiler, and dependency pins.
The COCO objective source revision is pinned: 52 of 53 source hashes reproduce
exactly; generated `coco_version.c` comments differ while the version string is
`2.8.3-dev8+gdd4bd1f0`. This difference is recorded, not represented as 53-file
byte identity. Full traces and gate logs stay in the supplemental evidence archive.

Use `quality_long.py` with the pinned replica, normalizers, seed lists above,
`--trials 3000` against label `baseline`, and `--trials 10000` against a reference
materialized by `full_history_reference.py`. Compile `identity.cpp` with the same
pinned replica include directory and core/COCO libraries; define
`PINEFORGE_HPO_LEGACY_TPE` only for the baseline and retain linker section garbage
collection. `identity_replay.py` compares all proposal/value bytes.
`profile.cpp` supports `default-startup` for the selection probe and `update` for
bounded batch-eight acquisition. `run_native.py` performs the million-trial full
billing parse. `collect_switch.py` publishes and verifies the compact evidence.
